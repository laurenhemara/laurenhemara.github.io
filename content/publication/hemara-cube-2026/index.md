abstract: 'Microbial inoculants could shift agriculture towards enhanced sustainability by reducing reliance on chemical fertilizers and pesticides, and enhancing soil and plant health in ways that synthetic inputs cannot. However, many inoculants suffer from unpredictable establishment and in-field function. To overcome this uptake barrier, we must focus on environmental compatibility - specifically with respect to conditions inoculants will be exposed to during industrial production. Current inoculant development efforts focus on desired plant growth-promoting traits. However, an effective inoculant must also have high fitness across highly variable production and application landscapes - including the industrial production pipeline and recipient fields. With 197 isolates from the recently-established Canadian Collection of Agricultural Soil Microbes - representing over 60 genera - we sought to evaluate microbial performance in several often-overlooked environments of the industrial pipeline: batch cultivation, storage, and in-soil application. We developed a growth-based framework to assess microbial performance across production stages, categorizing potential inoculants as industrial pipeline generalists or single-stage specialists. We observed diverse performance outcomes across environments. Interestingly, many isolates are pipeline generalists, including diverse taxa with industrial potential that is similar to (or better than) commonly commercialized taxa. This screening approach demonstrates the importance of explicitly evaluating environmental compatibility of candidate strains for inoculum development, given that these traits cannot be easily predicted from a microbe’s taxonomic identity. This research also demonstrates the likelihood of inoculant success without modification, identifying a number of pipeline generalists with strong potential for commercial development - particularly within the genera Pseudomonas and Arthrobacter. Ultimately, this framework provides a scalable approach to screen large culture collections for habitat generalists that are well suited to industrial production, across any biotechnology context where effective microbial products must be suited to diverse production and application environments.'
